# SHIELD-Summer-School
SHIELD Summer School Materials Repository
Authors: Nicholas Gross, Merav Opher, Chika Onubogu, Erick Powell, Marc Kornbleuth, Senbei Du, Heather Elliott, Parisa Mostafavi, Marc Swisdak, Maher Dayeh, Jonathan Gasser, and Jesse Miller

The SHIELD (Solar wind with Hydrogen Ion charge Exchange and Large-Scale Dynamics) DRIVE Science Center (https://shielddrivecenter.com) is a large collaboration that explores the physics of the heliospheric boundary, where the solar wind interacts with the interstellar medium and protects the inner solar system from the harsh interstellar environment. Along with these research goals, SHIELD is also training the next generation of scientists to study this environment. On SHIELD effort that does that is the SHIELD Summer School, titled “Plasma Processes at the Edge of the Solar System” (https://shielddrivecenter.com/shield-summer-school/). The summer school invites about 20 students each year for an intensive one-week course in the most challenging plasma processes that drive the structure and dynamics at the Edge of the Solar System.

During the SHIELD Summer School participants experience lectures from leading research scientists working in the outer heliosphere covering plasma processes that are important in understanding the structure of the heliosphere.  Participants also engage in activities that explore data from important missions such as  Voyager, New Horizons, and IBEX, as well as simulation results exploring magnetic reconnection.

This repository consists of lecture materials and lab activities from each day of the Summer School.The activities are embedded in Python notebooks developed for the Goggle Colab environment (which makes them easy to share and run). The notebooks include both background material, instructions, and code blocks that allow users to explore the data or simulation results to gain insight into the physical processes underlying the important processes in the outer heliosphere. The overall learning goals of the activities include:

- Increasing participant knowledge of the structures in the outer heliosphere
- Understanding and interpreting global radial trends in the plasma parameters in the outer heliosphere
- Understanding plasma processes such as magnetic reconnection and turbulence
- Being able to see the signatures of these processes in the spacecraft data

For the activities, each notebook is broken into small code blocks separated by explanation and questions. The questions are intended to prompt exploration.  Some code blocks are skeleton code and used to explore the concepts. If you are working on these activities with others then you should discuss your reasoning when answering the questions. If you are working on your own, take the time to write out your results and explanations. Separate notebooks are included with “Answers” showing some of the general features that could be explored.  

The repository is organized in folders containing materials and activities for each day of the Summer School.  

Day 1: Fundamentals of the Heliosphere 
Includes an introduction to the formation of the heliosphere and the structure of the boundary as well as fundamentals of plasma physics governing the boundary such as MHD and shocks. In the activity, users will explore Voyager data collected at different regions of the heliosphere and learn how this regions are defined and seperated.
Contributors: Chika Onubogu (BU), Erick Powell (BU), 
Marc Kornbleuth (BU), Senbei Du (BU)

Day 2: Variations in the Solar Wind Through the Solar System
Lecture materials from Heather Elliott (SWRI) and Parisa Mostafavi (JHU/APL) detail the origins of the solar wind and how it evolves as it expands into the inner and outer heliosphere. The activity primarily focuses on New Horizons data as it passes through the outer heliosphere and approaches the heliosphere termination shock where the solar wind slows down suddenly. The role of pick-up ions is also explored. 
Contributors: Heather Elliott (SWRI), Parisa Mostafavi (JHU/APL)

Day 3: Magnetic Reconnection and its Role in the Heliosphere
Lecture materials include an introduction to magnetic reconnection and techniques for simulating the process. The second part of the lecture is devoted to applications of these simulations to studying various phenomena including processes in the outer heliosphere.  
The activity explores simulation results of reconnection using Particle-In-Cell (PIC) simulation, and includes the effects of adding Pickup Ions (PUIs) to the plasma. 
Contributor: Marc Swisdak (Univ. of Maryland)

Day 4: Measuring Energenetic Neutrals 
Lecture materials cover the creation of Energetic Numerical Atoms (ENA’s) and their measurement by the IBEX and IMAP missions. The activity visualizes the data from the IBEX mission and creates all-sky maps of ENA measurements. These show features of the heliosphere 
Contributors: Maher Day (SWRI), Jonathan Gasser (SWRI)

Day 5: Heliosphere and Habitability
Lecture materials cover the effects that the heliosphere has on the habitability of the solar system. There is no activity associated with this lecture.  
Contributors: Jesse Miller (BU)
